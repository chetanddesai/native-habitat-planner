# Pruning Calendar — Poway Native Plants

Maintenance guide for ten Poway-area natives. **Most woody and herbaceous cutback happens in fall** (after seed for wildlife), with a shorter late-winter pass before spring growth. Only a few species need summer windows (oak pathogens; cool-season grass dormancy). You do **not** need pruning chores every month.

**Wildlife first:** delay deadheading and hard cutbacks until birds and beneficial insects have used seeds and standing stems.

---

## When the work actually happens

| Season | Load | What’s going on |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fall (Oct–Nov)** | **Heaviest** | Main rejuvenation after seed, before cool-season growth: white sage, bush sunflower, mugwort. |
| **Late winter (February)** | Moderate | Warm-season grass cutback; strawberry cleanup; Indian mallow tip pinch / light heading. |
| **Early–mid summer (Jun–Aug)** | Light / as needed | Ceanothus post-bloom; tidy tips after seed; purple needlegrass while summer-dormant; coast live oak in dry heat only. |
| **Mar–May, Sep, Dec** | **Usually idle** | Watch bloom/seed; optional tidy-tips deadheading in April. No required pruning rounds. |

Fall is the right default for chaparral shrubs. Exceptions that **should not** be piled into fall:

- *Sporobolus airoides* — leave seed heads through winter; cut in **February** (late winter, before green-up).
- *Abutilon palmeri* — primary tip pinch / light heading in **late winter**, not fall.
- *Quercus agrifolia* — prune only in **dry season** (Jul–Oct), not in wet weather.
- *Stipa pulchra* — cut in **summer dormancy** (Aug), before fall rains restart growth.
- *Ceanothus* ‘Frosty Blue’ — **June**, right after bloom finishes on a dry day (fall cuts remove next year’s flowers).
- *Salvia apiana* and *Encelia californica* — **fall only** (after seed, before cool-season growth); do not leave them for February.

## Pruning cut types

Use these cuts with the activities below. Match the cut to the goal — do not shear everything into the same shape.

| Cut | What you do | Typical activity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Heading cut** | Shorten a stem by cutting above a bud or leaf node, removing the terminal tip. Stimulates branching below the cut. | Heading, tip pinching, shearing (many heading cuts at once) |
| **2. Thinning cut** | Remove a branch entirely back to its origin (trunk, main stem, or ground). Opens the plant without stimulating a flush of tip growth. | Thinning, lifting/skirting (prune bottom-up), removing dead or crossing wood |
| **3. Reduction / releadering cut** | Cut a larger stem back to a strong lateral that can become the new leader (often ~⅓ of the branch). Shrinks size while keeping natural form. | Reduction (cut back ⅓), reshaping shrubs and oaks |
| **4. Jump cut** | Staged reduction of a girthy branch: first undercut and remove most of the weight farther out, then finish closer to the collar. Prevents bark tear-out on large limbs. | Jump cut on oaks and other woody plants with thick branches |

### Activity glossary

| Activity | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Thinning** | Remove selected stems at the origin; includes lifting/skirting (clear from the bottom up). |
| **Reduction** | Cut back about ⅓ to a strong lateral (releadering). |
| **Jump cut** | Two-step removal of girthy branches (see cut #4). |
| **Heading** | Head off a branch tip above a node. |
| **Shearing** | Many heading cuts at once for a uniform surface — use sparingly on natives. |
| **Tip pinching** | Soft pinch of new tip growth to thicken the plant. |
| **Deadheading** | Remove spent blooms (after wildlife has had seed access when possible). |
| **Coppicing & mowing** | Cut stems near or to the ground for full rejuvenation. |

## Work calendar (active months only)

| Month | Species | Pruning Activity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **October–November** *(primary fall round)* | • *Salvia apiana* (White sage)<br>• *Encelia californica* (CA bush sunflower)<br>• *Artemisia douglasiana* (Mugwort) | • **Deadheading + thinning / light reduction** (annual): After birds finish seed and **before winter growth** — shape only to leafy nodes; never into old leafless hardwood.<br>• **Reduction** (~⅓) or harder (annual): Cut rangy stems after seed shed and **before winter rains** green it up; do not wait until late winter.<br>• **Cut back to ground** (annual): Remove spent stems near the crown after seed; leave a patch standing into mid-winter if you want insect cover. |
| **February** *(late-winter round)* | • *Sporobolus airoides* (Alkali sacaton)<br>• *Abutilon palmeri* (Indian mallow)<br>• *Fragaria vesca* (Woodland strawberry) | • **Coppicing & mowing**: Cut warm-season bunchgrass to 2–4 in before spring green-up.<br>• **Tip pinching & light heading**: Primary shaping window for denser growth — softwood only.<br>• **Thinning**: Clear tattered winter foliage before flowers and berries start. |
| **June** | • *Ceanothus* ‘Frosty Blue’ (Frosty Blue California lilac)*<br>• *Layia platyglossa* (Tidy tips) | • **Tip pinching & thinning** (annual, right after Mar–May bloom on a dry day): Pinch green tips; thinning cuts for dead/crossing wood. Never cut into old wood without leaves. No other pruning this year.<br>• **Cut or pull spent annuals** after seed set: Plants do not resprout; next year’s stand comes from seed. *(Optional April deadheading only if you want a longer bloom display.)* |
| **July** | • *Quercus agrifolia* (Coast live oak) | • **Thinning, reduction & jump cuts** (as needed, often every few years): Dry-season structural work — crown thin, skirt/lift, releader long limbs; jump-cut girthy branches. Schedule anytime in the Jul–Oct dry window; no wet-season pruning. |
| **August** | • *Stipa pulchra* (*Nassella pulchra*, Purple needlegrass) | • **Coppicing & mowing** (annual or every 2–3 years): Cut cool-season bunchgrass while summer-dormant, after birds have used seed and before fall rains. |

\* *Ceanothus* ‘Frosty Blue’ is a California lilac cultivar, not a manzanita (*Arctostaphylos*). Post-bloom, green wood only.

**Idle months:** March–May (watch Ceanothus bloom; optional tidy-tips deadhead in April), September (seed still in use — wait), December (only finish leftover mugwort if the fall round slipped).

## Species notes — frequency, dormancy, bloom & wildlife

| Species | How often | Best window | Timing logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Salvia apiana* | **Annual** light work | **Fall** (Oct–Nov) | Cool-season grower; shape after seed, before winter flush; never hard into old wood. |
| *Fragaria vesca* | **Annual** light cleanup | **Late winter** | Before bloom/fruit; don’t shear off spring flowers. |
| *Ceanothus* ‘Frosty Blue’ | **Annual** once | **June** (post-bloom, dry day) | Blooms Mar–May; prune when flowers finish. Fall/winter cuts remove next year’s buds; wet weather raises canker risk. |
| *Layia platyglossa* | **Annual** lifecycle | **June** after seed (optional April deadhead) | Leave seed for birds/self-sow when possible. |
| *Abutilon palmeri* | **Annual** light shape | **Late winter** (Feb) | Desert Museum: late-winter prune or summer tip pinch; softwood only. |
| *Sporobolus airoides* | **Annual** cutback | **February** | Warm-season; leave seed through winter; cut late winter before early spring green-up. |
| *Stipa pulchra* | **Annual** or every **2–3 years** | **August** | Summer dormancy; before fall rains. |
| *Encelia californica* | **Annual** rejuvenation | **Fall** (Oct–Nov) | Cool-season grower; cut after seed, before winter rains — not late winter. |
| *Quercus agrifolia* | **As needed** | **Jul–Oct** (dry weather) | Dry-season pathogen window; acorns for wildlife. |
| *Artemisia douglasiana* | **Annual** hard cutback | **Fall** (or Dec finish) | After seed; standing stems shelter insects if delayed. |

### Reflection checklist (apply before cutting)

1. **Frequency** — One fall round + one late-winter pass covers almost everything; oak and needlegrass are the summer exceptions.
2. **Dormancy** — Warm-season grass in winter; cool-season grass in summer; oaks only in dry heat.
3. **Buds & bloom** — Never fall-prune Ceanothus; stop tip work once flower buds are set.
4. **Wildlife** — Fall work starts *after* seed use, not at the first brown stem.
